# What to Gather Before You Start

SCS builds your AI context from what you give it: documents you already have, and your answers
to a few questions. The better the material, the better the context. This page lists what
helps, so you can collect it before the first session.

You do not need all of it. Bring what you have; the assistant drafts from it, asks about the
rest, and leaves gaps for later. A draft policy, meeting notes, or a list in an email are all
fine. Documents stay on your computer.

## What makes context useful

AI context is read by an assistant in the middle of someone's work. It helps most when it says
what to **do** and what **never** to do in a situation, not only how the organization is
organized. When you gather material, look for:

- **Rules an assistant can act on.** "Never put client specifications into a public AI tool"
  helps. "Quality owns vendor qualification" informs people but changes nothing the assistant
  does.
- **What happens at the edges.** What should the assistant do when someone pastes client data
  into the wrong tool, or asks it to approve a record? Those answers prevent most problems.
- **What is in place today, and what is planned.** A process that is not running yet should not
  be described as if it were.
- **Your existing AI rules in any form.** If you already have an AI system prompt, a rule list,
  or instructions configured in an AI tool, bring them. They are the closest thing to finished
  context.

## Organization context (the Domain)

The Domain holds the rules that apply to all of your organization's work with AI tools.

| Topic | What to gather | Typical source |
|---|---|---|
| Who you are | What you make or do, services, sites, the kind of work most people do | Website, company overview |
| AI policy | Your AI use policy and any SOPs or standards that implement it | Policy, SOPs |
| Approved tools | Which AI tools are approved, for which kinds of data, and on which accounts | Tool register, IT approvals |
| Data | How information is classified, what may go into which tool, and what may never go into any | Data classification policy, confidentiality policy |
| Never | What AI tools must never be used for: decisions, approvals, kinds of data, kinds of work | Policy, handbook, leadership |
| Human review | Who reviews AI output before it is used, and when a second, independent reviewer is needed | Policy, quality procedures |
| Records | What must be logged when AI contributes to work, and which system is the record | Quality or records procedures |
| People | Who owns the AI policy and each area (security, quality, legal, HR), and who releases new versions of the context | Org chart, policy roles |
| Incidents | What counts as misuse, and how it is reported | Policy, incident procedure |

Also useful when they apply: employee handbook sections on confidentiality, recording,
acceptable use, and IT security; vendor contracts or terms for your AI tools; client contract
terms about AI use.

### If you are regulated

If you work under regulations or certifications (for example ISO 13485, 21 CFR Part 11,
HIPAA, SOC 2), also gather:

| Topic | What to gather |
|---|---|
| Scope | Which regulations and certifications apply, with editions, and to which sites or work |
| Electronic records | Which systems are validated or controlled, and what AI may and may not do in them |
| Validation | When an AI tool or AI-produced output needs qualification or verification, and by whom |
| Retention | How long AI-related records are kept |
| Risk | How risk files and risk decisions are handled when AI assists |

SCS records standards as references (for example `std:iso-13485:2016#7.3.9`), never their text,
so you do not need copies of the standards themselves.

## Personal context

For one person's own work, less is needed:

- what AI tools should never do on your work;
- habits you want applied everywhere, such as languages, testing, or writing style;
- the tools you use.

A few clear prohibitions are enough to start.

## Project context

A Project adds the rules for one piece of work. It inherits the organization's rules, so gather
only what is particular to the project.

| Topic | What to gather | Typical source |
|---|---|---|
| The work | What is being built or done, why, for whom, and what is out of scope for now | Project brief, charter, kickoff notes |
| Team | Who leads, who decides, who reviews, and who releases the project's context | Project plan |
| Data | What data the project touches, where it lives, and who may see it | Brief, IT or security review |
| Classification | Any regulatory or safety classification, and who decides it (for example whether software is part of the quality system) | Quality, regulatory |
| Technology | Languages, platforms, and the AI tools the team uses on it | Engineering notes |
| Project rules | Anything stricter than the organization's rules for this work | Team, client requirements |

## When you are ready

Put the documents in one folder you can reach from the AI tool. The assistant asks for them at
the start, gives you the exact place to copy them, and reads them before asking questions.
